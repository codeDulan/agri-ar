AgriAR: An Interactive WebXR Field-Monitoring Experience

A browser-based augmented-reality tool for precision agriculture that renders live weather on 3D farm assets, demonstrating marker-based and markerless tracking with a live-data advanced feature.

01 Problem & Motivation

Growers and field technicians make daily decisions — irrigation, spraying, turbine maintenance — from environmental conditions that vary hour to hour. Dedicated agri-tech dashboards exist but require app installation, accounts, and often paid hardware, which is a barrier for smallholders and for quick briefings in the field.

AgriAR is a zero-install AR experience that overlays live weather onto a small 3D farm (a wind turbine and a corn field). It answers one question — “what are conditions doing to this field right now, and to the turbine’s output?” — in two situations:

  • Marker mode — a printed marker on a field signboard, brochure, or equipment tag brings up a compact briefing that steps through three weather scenarios and their effect on the crop.
  • Markerless mode — the operator places a full farm model on the ground in front of them and walks around it while it reflects the actual live weather for any chosen location.

Why WebXR is the right medium

The experience is a single URL that runs on any modern phone with no download, works offline-first from a CDN, and is shareable by link or QR code. It reuses the camera, GPS, and motion sensors already in the device, and degrades gracefully to a 3D preview where immersive AR is unavailable. A native app would add friction disproportionate to the task.

02 Design

Information architecture

Three static pages share one component and asset library: a landing menu with a capability check, and one page per tracking mode. The modes are deliberately separated rather than toggled in a single scene because AR.js takes exclusive control of the renderer and camera feed in a way that is incompatible with an active WebXR immersive session on the same page. Separation also keeps each page’s payload small and each tracking path independently testable.

Scene & visual design

Both modes render the same farm: a wind turbine and an optimised corn field on a solid raised plot, lit by an ambient plus a directional light. The plot and the corn re-colour with the conditions (dry tan, lush green, or natural). A dark translucent data panel floats above the scene and always faces the viewer. Sky effects (a sun, or clouds with falling rain) appear above the field according to conditions. The palette is kept muted and the panel compact so the virtual content competes with reality, not with a blank page.

Asset strategy

Both 3D models are openly licensed and were optimised for web delivery rather than modelled from scratch (see §3). Audio is synthesised at runtime with the Web Audio API, so there are no media files to host or license.

Marker-based tracking

The scene declares arjs="sourceType: webcam; detectionMode: mono; ..." and anchors content inside <a-marker preset="hiro"> with pose smoothing (smoothCount: 8, smoothTolerance: 0.01) to reduce jitter. markerFound / markerLost events drive the status line, the scan hint, and a confirmation chime.

Markerless tracking

The scene requests webxr="requiredFeatures: hit-test; optionalFeatures: local-floor,dom-overlay" with the HUD as the DOM overlay so it stays visible during the session. ar-hit-test runs a hit-test against detected surfaces, shows a reticle, and on the user’s screen tap teleports the farm entity to the reticle and reveals it. A finite-state flow — scanning → surface achieved → placed → manipulating — governs which UI and interactions are active.

Because plane detection quality varies with lighting and surface texture, a fallback placement path was added: if no surface is reported within six seconds, the HUD invites the user to tap anywhere and the farm is dropped one metre ahead of the camera at an estimated floor height, computed from the camera pose. The hit-test path remains primary; the fallback only guarantees the experience is always usable.

Advanced feature — live data (Option A)

On the markerless page, weather.js resolves a location (device geolocation, a searched city via the Geocoding API, or a fallback coordinate) and polls the Open-Meteo Forecast API every five minutes for temperature_2m, weather_code, wind_speed_10m and wind_direction_10m. Each response is broadcast as a weather-updated event that reshapes the 3D content:

Live valueEffect on the 3D scene
Wind speedTurbine blade rotation speed (RPM), capped for realism
Wind directionTurbine nacelle yaws to face into the wind
WMO weather codeClear → sun; cloud/fog → clouds; drizzle/rain/storm → clouds + 140 instanced raindrops
Condition + temperatureCorn model and the ground field re-colour — dry tan under sun, lush green in rain, natural otherwise; ambient wind/rain audio level
All valuesOn-scene panel: location, temperature, wind vector, sky state

The same event contract is reused by the marker page: its three scenario buttons synthesise weather-updated records, so one rendering pipeline serves both live data and the scripted demo.

Complex interaction

Beyond clicks, the markerless mode provides multi-step spatial state management and direct manipulation: drag-to-rotate and pinch-to-scale the placed farm (touch and mouse), a reversible Re-place action that restores the default transform and re-arms surface scanning, and a sound toggle. The marker mode adds stateful scenario switching with an active-state indicator. The gesture handler is disabled until an object is actually placed and ignores any touch that begins on a HUD control.

Animation, lighting & audio

  • Animation — blade spin via tick(), an InstancedMesh rain system recycling 140 drops, a pulsing status LED, animated scene transitions between scenarios.
  • Lighting — ambient fill plus a directional key with shadows enabled.
  • Audio — looped low-passed noise for wind (level tracks wind speed), band-passed noise for rain, and an oscillator confirmation chime; the audio context is created on first user gesture to satisfy autoplay policy.

Asset optimisation

A scripted gltf-transform pipeline — dedup → prune → center → resize(512) → WebP(72) → weld → simplify(0.65) → Draco — was applied to both raw downloads. center --pivot below was essential: the raw turbine sat ~87 units off-origin.

Functional checks

  • Marker: content locks within ~1 s of the marker entering frame; each scenario button changes crop colour, sky, blade speed, and panel; chime fires; content hides on markerLost.
  • Markerless: the reticle appears only on a detected surface and a tap places the farm at real scale; where the surface is not detected, the six-second fallback lets the user tap to place. Once placed, the farm stays anchored while walking around; drag/pinch behave consistently; Re-place resets the transform and re-scans.
  • Live data: searching “Colombo”, “London”, “Reykjavik” updates the panel, sky, turbine yaw and blade speed within one poll; an invalid city shows “City not found”.
  • Resilience: denied camera → explained; denied location → silent fallback; offline API → “Live weather unavailable” with last reading kept; missing model → primitive placeholder.

Performance

Total transferred payload is ≈ 1.4 MB (A-Frame + AR.js + two models + scripts), most of it cached after first load. The scene holds interactive frame rates on the test Android device; the instanced rain system adds a single draw call. Note your observed fps / load time.

Known limitations

  • Markerless AR is unavailable on iOS Safari, and WebXR plane detection needs a reasonably lit, textured surface; on the Pixel 7 Pro test unit the native hit-test was unreliable and the tap-to-place fallback carried the demo.
  • On the Pixel 7 Pro, Chrome opens the front camera for the AR.js (marker) page despite an environment facing-mode request; the marker still tracks, and the page works correctly on other cameras and on desktop.
  • Typing a city inside an active WebXR session is unreliable under dom-overlay; the city is best set before entering AR.
  • Shadows are approximate; there is no real-world light estimation.
  • The 3-minute demonstration video is recorded separately and submitted alongside this report.

05 Usability Evaluation — Nine AR Heuristics

Evaluated against Nielsen & Molich’s heuristics as re-framed for augmented reality (“when the interface is the room”).

1Visibility of status

A persistent status line reports every state — starting camera, searching for marker, marker locked, scanning, surface found, farm placed, refreshing weather. The menu’s capability check states support up front.

2Match the real world

Models sit on the detected plane or marker at real-world scale and behave plausibly — the turbine faces the wind and spins faster in stronger wind; the crop and the field dry under sun and green in rain, matching what the numbers say.

3Control & freedom

Re-place undoes a bad placement and resets transform; scenarios switch freely; every page has a Menu button; gesture manipulation is fully reversible.

4Consistency

One rendering pipeline and one HUD across both modes. A gesture has a single meaning — drag always rotates, pinch always scales — and manipulation exists only where a placed object exists.

5Error prevention

The reticle appears only on a real detected surface, so the primary tap can’t place into thin air; the fallback is offered only after scanning has clearly failed. The gesture handler stays off until something is placed and ignores touches that begin on a control, preventing accidental transforms.

6Recognition, not recall

Every action is an on-screen control — scenario bar, Refresh, Re-place, sound. The one gesture set is spelled out on screen (“Drag to rotate · pinch to resize”). Nothing is a hidden command.

7Minimalist design

Translucent compact panel, single-line readout in markerless mode, controls hidden until relevant, sky effects scaled down after review. Content stays subordinate to the camera view.

8Recover from errors

Messages say what to do: “Camera blocked: NotAllowedError — enable it in the address bar”, “No WebXR — open on Android Chrome”, “Live weather unavailable — showing default scene”.

9Help & documentation

Help is in the experience: a scan hint with a link to print the Hiro marker, inline gesture hints, and the capability check that explains any gap. No external manual is needed.

06 Technical Challenges & Solutions

The specific engineering problems met during development and the exact fixes applied.

1 · Turbine model floated far from the marker

Symptom
The turbine rendered metres away from the marker and off-screen.
Cause
The FBX2glTF-exported model had its origin ~87 units from its geometry; A-Frame places entities at their origin.
Fix
Added gltf-transform center --pivot below to the asset pipeline, moving the origin to the base of the mesh so it sits exactly on the marker/plane.

2 · The whole turbine rotated instead of just the blades

Symptom
Animating rotation on the entity spun the tower and blades together — physically wrong.
Cause
The animation acts on the entity’s root Object3D, not on an inner part.
Fix
Wrote the spin-part component: on model-loaded it looks up the blade sub-object by name (getObjectByName('windturbine_tall_fan')) and rotates only that in tick(). On a name miss it logs every mesh name in the model, which is how the correct name was found.

3 · AR.js camera feed letterboxed on phones, plus a stray “VR” button

Symptom
On a portrait phone the camera preview showed black bars, and A-Frame’s enter-VR button appeared over the AR view.
Cause
AR.js sizes its <video> to the camera’s native landscape resolution; A-Frame injects an XR-mode button by default.
Fix
xr-mode-ui="enabled: false" plus CSS forcing the AR.js video and canvas to position: fixed; width/height: 100%; object-fit: cover with !important, overriding the inline dimensions.

4 · Marker and markerless tracking could not share one page

Symptom
Combining AR.js and a WebXR immersive session in one scene broke the renderer and camera.
Cause
AR.js assumes exclusive control of the Three.js renderer and video source.
Fix
A three-page architecture: a shared menu plus one isolated page per mode, sharing a common component and asset library. This also halved each page’s script payload and made each tracking path independently testable.

5 · Ground plane shimmered against the crop model

Symptom
A flickering seam where the field plane met the corn model’s own base, worst at grazing angles.
Cause
Z-fighting between two coplanar semi-transparent surfaces at the same depth.
Fix
Replaced the transparent plane with a solid box sunk just below ground level and lifted the crop a few millimetres, so the surfaces never coincide.

6 · Corn field model was 2 MB — too heavy for mobile AR

Symptom
First-load stall on mobile data; the model dominated the page weight.
Cause
A single 1024px PNG base-colour texture accounted for most of the file.
Fix
Pipeline resize to 512px, WebP quality 72, mesh decimation to 65%, then Draco — 2.09 MB → 63 KB (97% smaller) with no visible quality loss at AR scale.

7 · Live weather was almost always “clear” during testing

Symptom
Rain and stress states could not be verified on demand.
Cause
Real conditions at the test location rarely changed.
Fix
Added ?condition= and ?wind= URL overrides for deterministic testing and demos, and made the marker mode’s scenario buttons emit the same synthetic weather records — turning a testing aid into a product feature.

8 · Audio would not start; asset-licensing overhead

Symptom
Silent playback, and sourcing/attributing sound files added friction.
Cause
Browser autoplay policy blocks audio before a user gesture.
Fix
Synthesised all audio with the Web Audio API (filtered noise for wind/rain, an oscillator chime) and created the audio context lazily on the first tap of the sound button — no files, no licensing, policy satisfied.

9 · gltf-transform global install was blocked; camera on file://

Symptom
npm i -g failed with an OS permission error; opening pages directly gave no camera.
Cause
Restricted global node_modules; getUserMedia and fetch require a secure context.
Fix
Ran the CLI via npx --yes @gltf-transform/cli@4; served locally over localhost and deployed to GitHub Pages (HTTPS) for on-device testing.

10 · Desktop mouse-drag rotated both the camera and the farm

Symptom
Dragging in the preview spun the view and the model at once.
Cause
A-Frame’s default look-controls and the new gesture-transform both consumed the drag.
Fix
Disabled look-controls — this is an AR app whose camera pose comes from the device — and had gesture-transform ignore any gesture that begins on a UI element.

11 · AR.js opened the selfie camera on the Pixel 7 Pro

Symptom
The marker page consistently started on the front camera on one Android device, though it was correct on desktop and on other phones.
Cause
Two contributing factors: a pre-flight getUserMedia call used only to detect permission was opened and closed immediately before AR.js opened its own stream, and Android Chrome then re-acquired the default (front) camera; the device also appears to ignore a non-exact facingMode: environment hint.
Attempts that failed
Injecting a resolved rear deviceId into the arjs config after scene load left AR.js uninitialised (blank screen); swapping the MediaStream on AR.js’s <video> at runtime broke its AR controller on mobile. Both were reverted.
Resolution
Removed the pre-flight getUserMedia so AR.js performs a single clean camera request. This is a documented device-specific browser limitation rather than a project defect — marker tracking itself is unaffected.

12 · WebXR hit-test produced no reticle on the test device

Symptom
The AR session started and the camera showed, but the status stayed on “Scanning for a surface…” — ar-hit-test-achieved never fired.
Cause
Two issues: local-floor was listed as a required feature, which can make a session request fail on devices that do not grant it; and the environment/surface was marginal for ARCore plane detection.
Fix
Relaxed requiredFeatures to hit-test only (with local-floor optional), and added a resilient fallback: six seconds after entering AR with no surface, the user can tap anywhere to place the farm one metre ahead of the camera at an estimated floor height, derived from the live camera pose. The hit-test path stays primary.

13 · Hidden HUD controls stayed visible and overlapped the hint text

Symptom
The Re-place / Weather button bar rendered on top of the “Press Enter AR…” instruction before anything was placed.
Cause
The bar was hidden with the HTML hidden attribute, but the rule .controls { display: flex } has higher specificity than the user-agent [hidden] rule and overrode it.
Fix
Added an explicit [hidden] { display: none !important; } rule, then rebuilt the bottom overlay as two clearly separated rows with env(safe-area-inset-*) padding so nothing collides or sits under the phone’s home indicator. The status pill now truncates instead of pushing the layout.

14 · Crop tint silently stopped working in markerless mode

Symptom
The corn model changed colour with the weather on the marker page but never on the markerless page.
Cause
An earlier revert of an abandoned feature also removed the crop selector from the markerless weather-fx declaration; there was no error because the selector is optional.
Fix
Restored the selector and, at the same time, extended weather-fx to also re-colour the ground field, caught during a side-by-side test of the two modes.

07 Reflection

What worked. Committing early to one weather-event contract meant the sky, turbine, crop and audio all reacted to a single source of truth, and the marker demo fell out of it for free. The asset pipeline as a checked-in script made model swaps painless — the corn field was replaced late in development in minutes. Deploying to GitHub Pages on day one made on-device testing continuous rather than a final-week scramble.

What I would change. The two modes drifted toward being the same scene twice before I gave them distinct jobs; deciding the split first would have saved rework. I also spent too long fighting AR.js internals over the front-camera issue before accepting it as a device-specific browser constraint — testing on the target device earlier would have set that expectation. On the markerless side, the fallback placement should have been designed in from the start rather than added under pressure when hit-test proved unreliable on the test unit. Further work: WebXR light estimation for believable shadows, a small offline cache so a dropped connection during a field visit keeps the last reading, and a second Android device in the test matrix.

Against the learning outcomes. The project applies core AR concepts (fiducial and markerless tracking, pose smoothing, hit-testing, real-world scale), builds an interactive WebXR experience on standard frameworks, implements meaningful interaction with 3D content (placement, direct manipulation, data-driven state), and evaluates the result across devices and against recognised usability heuristics.
